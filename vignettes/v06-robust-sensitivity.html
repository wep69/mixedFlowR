1 Purpose

Robust analysis is most useful when it is framed as a sensitivity analysis: would the scientific conclusion change if influential observations or clusters received less leverage under a robust estimator?

The robustlmm backend implements robust linear mixed-model estimation (Koller 2016). mixedFlowR also supports influence screens, classical-versus-robust comparisons, contamination summaries, and cluster-robust covariance through optional backends.

2 1. Learning objectives

You will learn to:

  • fit a robust Gaussian mixed model;
  • compare classical and robust fixed-effect estimates;
  • identify residual and cluster-level sensitivity;
  • distinguish influence from data error;
  • use cluster-robust covariance as another sensitivity tool;
  • construct a transparent contamination analysis;
  • report robust results without silently replacing the primary model.

12 11. What to do after a flag

A defensible sequence is:

  1. check the original field sheet, sensor file, or laboratory record;
  2. verify units and data entry;
  3. identify whether the value is physically plausible;
  4. determine whether the observation belongs to a special but valid condition;
  5. compare primary and sensitivity analyses;
  6. document any exclusion criterion independently of the desired statistical result.

15 14. Robust analysis does not solve model misspecification

If residuals show a strong nonlinear time trend, omitted treatment interaction, or serial dependence, downweighting unusual observations does not correct the mean or covariance model.

Robustness should therefore come after the core design and structural modeling decisions.

16 15. A three-model sensitivity panel

A useful report can compare:

  • classical LMM estimate;
  • robust LMM estimate;
  • classical estimate with cluster-robust covariance.

These methods address different vulnerabilities and should not be described as interchangeable.

19 18. Reporting template

A transparent sensitivity paragraph should state:

  1. why robust analysis was performed;
  2. estimator/backend used;
  3. whether influential observations or clusters were identified;
  4. how classical and robust estimates differed;
  5. whether the primary biological conclusion changed;
  6. whether any observation was excluded and why;
  7. whether cluster-robust covariance altered uncertainty materially.

21 20. Key take-home messages

  • Robust estimation is a sensitivity tool, not a license to ignore model structure.
  • Influence and contamination flags trigger review, not automatic deletion.
  • Classical and robust estimates should be compared transparently.
  • Cluster-robust covariance and robust mixed-model fitting address different inferential vulnerabilities.
  • Exclusions require an independently defensible scientific or data-quality reason.
