1 Purpose

Agronomic field trials are rarely spatially homogeneous. Soil depth, fertility, moisture, slope, compaction, previous management, and microrelief can create trends across rows and columns. If treatment allocation is superimposed on these gradients, ignoring spatial structure can reduce precision or confound treatment interpretation.

mixedFlowR provides two complementary spatial routes:

  1. parametric spatial covariance through glmmTMB;
  2. two-dimensional P-spline field correction through SpATS (Rodriguez-Alvarez et al. 2018).

2 1. Learning objectives

You will learn to:

  • map raw field observations before modeling;
  • distinguish block effects from continuous spatial trend;
  • fit exponential, Gaussian, and Matérn spatial covariance structures;
  • fit a two-dimensional P-spline field model with SpATS;
  • extract and visualize the spatial surface;
  • compare raw and spatially adjusted interpretations;
  • recognize spatial-treatment confounding;
  • report spatial models transparently.

4 3. Raw field map

if (requireNamespace("ggplot2", quietly = TRUE)) {
  ggplot2::ggplot(s, ggplot2::aes(col, row, fill = yield)) +
    ggplot2::geom_tile() +
    ggplot2::scale_fill_viridis_c() +
    ggplot2::coord_equal() +
    ggplot2::scale_y_reverse() +
    ggplot2::labs(
      x = "Field column",
      y = "Field row",
      fill = "Yield",
      title = "Observed field response"
    ) +
    ggplot2::theme_minimal(base_size = 11)
}

How to read the figure. Look for broad gradients, patches, row/column bands, local extremes, and whether treatment layout might align with the gradient. This map is descriptive and mixes treatment signal with spatial variation.

6 5. Parametric spatial covariance

Fit an exponential covariance structure.

if (requireNamespace("glmmTMB", quietly = TRUE)) {
  m_exp <- mixed_spatial_covariance(
    data      = s,
    response  = "yield",
    fixed     = "genotype + block",
    x         = "col",
    y         = "row",
    structure = "exp"
  )

  m_exp
}

14 13. Spatial-treatment confounding

A spatial smoother can be dangerous if treatment layout and field trend are nearly confounded.

Ask:

  • Are treatments randomized throughout the field?
  • Do particular genotypes occupy one region?
  • Is block layout aligned with the main gradient?
  • Does spatial correction erase a treatment pattern because the design cannot distinguish them?

No statistical method can fully recover treatment effects that were not separated from spatial position by design.

15 14. Block effects and spatial trend can coexist

Blocking accounts for designed large-scale heterogeneity. Spatial trend models residual field variation within and across blocks.

Do not drop blocks merely because a smooth spatial surface is fitted. Keep design terms that represent the actual randomization unless there is a clearly justified alternative model.

16 15. Field-trial analysis workflow

  1. inspect field layout and treatment map;
  2. plot raw response by row/column;
  3. audit blocks and treatment replication;
  4. fit a design-respecting nonspatial baseline;
  5. inspect residual spatial pattern;
  6. fit a plausible spatial covariance or surface;
  7. diagnose convergence and residual pattern;
  8. compare treatment/genotype estimates before and after correction;
  9. communicate both the field surface and adjusted treatment inference.

  1. Compare exponential and Gaussian covariance structures.
  2. Change the P-spline nseg values and inspect the fitted spatial trend.
  3. Fit genotype as fixed and random, and explain how the inferential target changes.
  4. Plot the treatment allocation itself across the field and discuss potential confounding.

20 19. Key take-home messages

  • Map the field before fitting a spatial model.
  • Spatial correction should represent unexplained field variation, not absorb treatment layout.
  • Blocks and spatial trends serve different roles.
  • Parametric covariance and P-spline smoothing are alternative modeling strategies, not identical models.
  • Show the spatial surface and adjusted treatment/genotype results separately.
