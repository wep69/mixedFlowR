1 Purpose

Multi-environment trials (METs) evaluate genotypes across environments, years, locations, or management conditions. The central statistical problem is not simply whether environments differ; it is how genotype performance changes across environments and how information should be borrowed across the resulting covariance structure.

mixedFlowR delegates MET and GxE estimation to sommer, a mixed-model solver designed for complex covariance and quantitative-genetic applications (Covarrubias-Pazaran 2016).

2 1. Learning objectives

You will learn to:

  • audit genotype-by-environment replication;
  • fit a baseline MET model;
  • distinguish environment fixed effects from genotype random effects;
  • fit diagonal, unstructured, and reduced-rank factor-analytic GxE covariance structures;
  • understand covariance borrowing across environments;
  • recognize overparameterization in unstructured GxE;
  • interpret reduced-rank GxE cautiously;
  • report MET results beyond a single overall genotype mean.

11 10. What factor-analytic structure means

A factor-analytic covariance represents GxE through a smaller set of latent covariance dimensions.

Conceptually:

  • environments have loadings on latent factors;
  • genotypes have factor scores;
  • the product helps reconstruct covariance in performance;
  • residual environment-specific GxE variance can remain.

Do not automatically interpret a latent factor as rainfall, temperature, or soil fertility unless external environmental covariates support that interpretation.

12 11. Stability is multidimensional

A genotype can have:

  • high overall mean and high interaction;
  • moderate mean and stable response;
  • specific adaptation to one environment class;
  • crossover interaction with other genotypes.

A single stability score can hide these distinctions.

16 15. Prediction and ranking caution

BLUP-like genotype predictions are conditional on:

  • fitted covariance structure;
  • environment representation;
  • residual model;
  • genotype fixed/random assumption;
  • relationship matrices if supplied in more advanced sommer models.

Do not report ranks without uncertainty or stability context.

17 16. Extending to pedigree/genomic covariance

The mixed_met() wrapper can pass advanced sommer random-effect structures through .... Such models should be documented carefully because known relationship matrices change the interpretation of genotype covariance.

A generic template is shown but not evaluated:

# Define `genomic_random_formula` from a scientifically justified
# sommer relationship structure before fitting.
fit_genomic <- mixed_met(
  fixed = yield ~ environment,
  random = genomic_random_formula,
  data = met_with_relationships,
  solver = "mmes"
)

18 17. Reporting checklist

Report:

  • environments and their sampling interpretation;
  • replication within environments;
  • genotype fixed/random status;
  • residual covariance assumption;
  • GxE covariance structure;
  • number of factors in reduced-rank models;
  • convergence diagnostics;
  • genotype predictions and uncertainty;
  • evidence of crossover or specific adaptation;
  • environmental covariates used to interpret latent factors, if any.

19 18. Exercises

  1. Fit diagonal and unstructured GxE models and compare parameter complexity.
  2. Fit FA1 and FA2 and explain what additional latent dimension is being allowed.
  3. Remove several genotype-environment cells and inspect support before refitting.
  4. Plot raw genotype profiles and identify examples of crossover interaction.

20 19. Key take-home messages

  • MET analysis is primarily about genotype performance across environments, not environment means alone.
  • GxE covariance structure determines how information is borrowed across environments.
  • Unstructured covariance can become parameter-heavy quickly.
  • Factor-analytic factors are statistical dimensions until externally interpreted.
  • Ranking genotypes without uncertainty and interaction context is incomplete.
