1 Purpose

This capstone vignette integrates the major concepts of the tutorial series in one coherent agronomic analysis. The example is a randomized complete-block split-plot trial with variety in whole plots, nitrogen dose in subplots, and yield as the response.

The purpose is not to use every advanced model. The purpose is to show when each extension becomes relevant and when the simpler analysis should be retained.

2 1. Learning objectives

You will learn to execute a complete workflow:

  1. define the scientific question;
  2. audit the randomization and experimental units;
  3. explore raw data;
  4. use legacy ANOVA as a design check;
  5. fit the design-correct LMM;
  6. diagnose the fit;
  7. interpret qualitative and quantitative effects;
  8. evaluate uncertainty;
  9. conduct robust and bootstrap sensitivity analyses;
  10. produce publication-ready figures and tables;
  11. generate a reproducibility record;
  12. decide whether advanced GAMLSS/Bayesian/spatial extensions are justified.

3 2. Scientific question

Assume the study asks:

Do varieties differ in grain yield, how does yield respond to nitrogen dose, and does the nitrogen response depend on variety when the split-plot randomization is represented correctly?

Secondary questions are:

  • Are conclusions sensitive to influential observations?
  • Are uncertainty intervals stable under bootstrap resampling?
  • Is there evidence that a more complex distributional or covariance model is needed?

5 4. Define the estimands before fitting

Variety: qualitative whole-plot treatment.
Nitrogen: quantitative subplot treatment.
Variety × nitrogen: modification of dose response by variety.
Block and block-by-variety: randomization-induced grouping components.

This declaration prevents the analysis from drifting into arbitrary pairwise tests or automatic model selection.

Questions to ask before modeling:

  • Is response plausibly smooth with dose?
  • Does one block behave differently?
  • Are there isolated extreme observations?
  • Does variance change with dose?
  • Are the tested doses dense enough to support curvature?

27 26. Is GAMLSS needed?

Do not fit GAMLSS automatically. First ask whether residual scale or shape remains systematically related to treatment or dose after the core mean and covariance structure is adequate.

A sensitivity template:

if (requireNamespace("gamlss", quietly = TRUE)) {

    yield ~ variety * nitrogen,
    d,
    family = "NO",
    random = "block"
  )

  g_scale <- mixed_gamlss(
    yield ~ variety * nitrogen,
    d,
    family = "NO",
    sigma.formula = ~ variety,
    random = "block"

  mixed_gamlss_select(

35 34. Interpretation framework

A final narrative should follow this sequence.

35.1 Design

State that variety was randomized to whole plots and nitrogen to subplots within blocks.

35.2 Model

State the fixed variety-by-nitrogen mean structure and random block/whole-plot components.

35.3 Diagnostics

Report convergence, singularity, and residual adequacy.

35.4 Inferential result

Describe treatment-specific nitrogen response, uncertainty, and any important interaction.

35.5 Sensitivity

State whether robust or bootstrap analyses changed the substantive conclusion.

35.6 Limits

Do not extrapolate beyond tested nitrogen range or interpret a grid optimum as an economic recommendation without cost/price modeling.

36 35. Decision points for escalation

Escalate only if needed.

Diagnostic/scientific problem Extension
residual heteroscedasticity mixed_heterogeneity() or distributional scale model
serial repeated measurements mixed_ar1() / mixed_car1()
count response mixed_count()
contamination sensitivity mixed_robust()
difficult interval uncertainty mixed_boot()
changing scale/shape mixed_gamlss()
posterior probability/prior question mixed_bayes()
nonlinear mechanistic response mixed_growth() / mixed_dose_response()
field spatial trend mixed_spatial_field()
multi-environment adaptation mixed_gxe()

37 36. What a complete analysis should not look like

Avoid:

  1. fitting every available engine;
  2. ranking all models by AIC;
  3. reporting only the smallest p-value;
  4. hiding convergence warnings;
  5. deleting influential data without provenance review;
  6. presenting EMM letters for quantitative dose as the main result;
  7. showing only bars and standard errors;
  8. interpreting a Bayesian credible interval as a frequentist CI;
  9. treating spatial smoothing as a correction that is always beneficial.

40 39. Key take-home messages

  • A complete mixed-model analysis begins with randomization, not software.
  • Mean structure, covariance, distribution, and robustness are separate modeling questions.
  • Quantitative treatments should preserve their scientific scale when possible.
  • Advanced modules are escalation paths, not mandatory steps.
  • Observed data, uncertainty, diagnostics, and reproducibility should remain visible in the final report.
